# Chapter 6: Prompts for Content Creation

## Core Idea
Content prompts work when creator niche, audience, format pair (e.g. video + blog), sponsor/product constraints, and engagement goals are specified—reuse mentorship and calendar patterns from earlier chapters.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Sponsored Tutorial + Companion Blog**: Food-blogger (or niche creator) produces a visually educational video tutorial *and* a complementary blog post.
  - When to use: Sponsored or product-led content needing education + conversion soft-sell.
  - How: Assign niche specialist; focus on one ingredient/topic; highlight sponsor gadget benefits (simplify, save time, elevate result); define audience; require both video script/tutorial and blog.
- **Multi-Personality Content Mentorship**: Same legendary-personality pattern as Ch 3, applied to engagement problems; multiple thinkers in one prompt.
  - When to use: Stuck engagement despite “good” production quality.
  - How: List personalities (e.g. Jobs, Disney, Spielberg); dump detailed doubt; require each voice’s mental models + one actionable step each.
- **Social Media Content Calendar (creator ops)**: Marketing-manager role builds mix of curated + original posts for a niche blogger with demographic psychographics, visuals, trends, seasonal events, hashtags.
  - When to use: Need a reusable posting mind map, not a single caption.
  - How: State niche (e.g. sustainable fashion), goal (engagement + followers), audience attributes, content mix, research/trend steps, hashtag use.

## Key Concepts
- **Dual-format deliverable**: Video tutorial + blog that adds context.
- **Sponsor integration**: Product benefits demonstrated inside the narrative, not bolted on.
- **Curated vs original mix**: Calendar balance for sustainability and brand voice.
- **Audience psychographics**: Age, values (e.g. conscious consumption), adjacent interests (wellness, travel).
- **Engagement diagnosis**: Separate craft quality from distribution/promotion strategy.

## Mental Models
- Use **video + blog pairing** when one asset must educate and another must deepen SEO/context.
- Use **multi-personality mentorship** when you want contrasting creative philosophies on the same doubt.
- Use a **calendar with curated/original mix** when posting fatigue kills consistency.
- Specify **niche + audience + seasonal hooks** when ideas feel repetitive.
- Think of hashtags as **visibility constraints** you design into the prompt, not afterthoughts.

## Anti-patterns
- **Sponsor mention without demonstration job**: “Mention product X” with no simplify/save-time/elevate criteria.
- **Engagement panic without promotion strategy**: Assuming better craft alone fixes reach.
- **Calendar without audience definition**: Generic posts for nobody in particular.
- **Single-personality advice when you need creative range**: One lens may miss distribution or storytelling angles.

## Worked Example
**Instagram recipe tutorial + blog** (compact reconstruction):

| Slot | Fill |
|------|------|
| Role | Food blogger: healthy, sustainable cooking |
| Goal | Visually stunning + educational Instagram video tutorial |
| Focus | Specific ingredient + kitchen-gadget sponsor benefits |
| Demo criteria | Simplify cooking, save time, elevate taste/presentation |
| Extra | Engaging complementary blog post |
| Audience | Food enthusiasts seeking new ingredients/gadgets |

**Transfer use cases from the book**: travel blogger + luggage sponsor; beauty influencer + eco makeup; fitness influencer + home-workout gadget—same skeleton, swapped niche/sponsor.

**Personality mentorship**: Filmmaker with solid craft but weak engagement asks Jobs/Disney/Spielberg; each answer ends with one action (e.g. sharpen emotional hook, story world, or promotion habit).

## Key Takeaways
1. Content prompts need niche expertise, audience, and format(s) declared.
2. Sponsor content should force product *demonstration criteria*, not name-drops.
3. Multi-voice mentorship yields diverse strategies ending in actions.
4. Calendars with curated/original mix and seasonal hooks sustain posting.
5. Reuse Ch 3–5 anatomy; only swap domain variables.
